== What and why

The author decided on 2026-11-12 to reach herdr-manifold from the phone as
well as from the desktops. A desktop application alone cannot serve that,
and messaging between agents
(link:../herdr-manifold%235-machines-and-messaging/spec.adoc[herdr-manifold#5])
must work while every window is closed. Both need a process that runs on
its own: a *hub*.

The hub holds the core of
link:../herdr-manifold%231-purpose-and-stack/spec.adoc[herdr-manifold#1],
reaches the Herdr servers of all machines, keeps the messages, and serves
the user interface as a web app. A browser on a desktop or a phone, the
Electron application and the VS Code extension are all front ends of the
same app.

This specification decides the shape: where hubs run, how front ends reach
them, how the phone gets in, and what the phone shows. It changes no code.

== Decision of 2026-11-12: Ovis for now

Ovis v0.5.3 (see "Prior art") was installed on ws1 the same day and
compared with the requirements. The author found that it covers most of
the need: the phone over NetBird with a certificate from Let's Encrypt,
the machines in one view, chat and live terminal for agent panes, images
pasted as files, Web Push, updates from its own settings. The author
decided to live on Ovis and to put this specification and the client of
herdr-manifold#1 on hold; gaps are collected while Ovis is in use.

The gaps known at the time of the decision:

* *No messaging between agents* (herdr-manifold#5): Ovis delivers text only
  by typing it into a pane. Its queue `server/pending-input.ts` is close to
  the wake-up of an idle agent and may serve a broker that runs next to
  Ovis.
* *No projects across machines*: Ovis groups machine → workspace → pane.
* *One server*: Ovis runs on ws1 and reaches the other machines by SSH;
  there is no second hub to move to (criterion K3 "Failover").

The installation on ws1, as it stands at the decision:

* A systemd user service `ovis` with `MemoryMax=6G`; Ovis listens on
  `127.0.0.1:7317` and requires its token; telemetry is off.
* Caddy listens on ws1's NetBird address, port 443, and proxies to Ovis;
  the certificate for `ws1.nb.kovrov.space` is obtained by DNS-01 with
  lego and renewed by a timer. This is directions 6 and 7 below, tried on
  one machine.
* Updates by Ovis's own updater, from the branch `main` of its clone.

The direction below stays as written, for the case that the gaps turn out
to need a client of our own.

== Direction

. *A hub on every machine, all alike.* `herdr-manifold serve` runs as a
  systemd user service on ws1, ws2, ws3, ws4, ws5, ws7 and ws9 (launchd on
  ws9). No hub is the main one: each reaches every Herdr server of the
  catalog of machines (Herdr's own, `endpoints.json`) by
  `remote-client-bridge` and `remote-api-bridge` over SSH, and its own
  machine by the local socket. This is the rule "machines are equal" of
  section 8.1 of the requirements, applied to the client.
+
The API channel (`remote-api-bridge` or `herdr.sock`) of every machine
stays connected: the sidebar, the phone and the delivery of messages need
the agents of all machines. The surface channel of a machine is open only
while some front end shows it, and is closed 30 s after the last one
leaves. With seven machines a hub on each makes 49 connections; keeping
their surfaces open would make every Herdr server render tabs for clients
nobody looks at, and how Herdr shares pane sizes between clients is not
established.
. *The core runs in the hub.* The core of herdr-manifold#1 already takes an
  injected byte transport and talks to the UI through a bridge; in the hub
  the transport is the SSH bridge or the local socket, and the bridge to
  the UI is a WebSocket. The core's rule "no Electron, VS Code, Node or DOM
  imports" stays.
. *The UI is a web app served by every hub.* The Svelte UI of
  herdr-manifold#1 is built once and served by the hub over HTTPS. The
  Electron application becomes a thin shell that opens the local hub's app
  and adds what a browser cannot: shortcuts a browser keeps for itself
  (`ctrl+w`, `ctrl+t`, `ctrl+n`), the system clipboard, a window of its
  own. The VS Code extension does the same in a webview.
+
Front ends on the hub's own machine may connect through a Unix socket,
`$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/herdr-manifold/hub.sock`, readable by the owner only.
A Flatpak without network permission has a loopback of its own and cannot
reach `127.0.0.1` of the machine; a socket in the file system it can, by
`--filesystem=xdg-run/herdr-manifold`.
. *A desktop uses its own machine's hub*, so a keystroke costs what it
  costs in herdr-manifold#1: the SSH round trip to the target machine plus
  the local bound, with no extra hop.
. *The phone joins the NetBird network* of the machines and opens any hub
  at `https://wsN.nb.kovrov.space:<port>`. The app knows every hub from the
  catalog; when the one it uses stops answering, it moves to the next in
  the author's order (ws1 … ws9) and says so.
. *Hubs listen on the NetBird interface and on loopback only*, never on a
  public address. Over that, every request is authenticated (method open
  below): a hub gives the terminals of every machine, which is remote code
  execution for whoever reaches it.
. *A certificate from Let's Encrypt by DNS-01* for `*.nb.kovrov.space`: the
  challenge is a TXT record `_acme-challenge.nb.kovrov.space` in the public
  zone `kovrov.space`, while the names themselves resolve to NetBird's
  private addresses. The certificate is valid on any phone without an own
  CA, and the hubs stay unreachable from outside. HTTPS is required anyway:
  over plain HTTP to a non-local address the browser gives no secure
  context — no service worker, no installable app, no Web Push, no reading
  of the clipboard.
. *The phone's view is not the pane grid.* A cell grid of 80 columns does
  not read on a phone. Its main view is:
  * the agents of all machines with their states (`<machine>/<pane>`
    addresses of herdr-manifold#5);
  * the threads and states of messages, sending a message to an agent;
  * pending approvals — permission requests relayed by Claude Code channels
    and approval requests of Codex — answered with one tap; both depend on
    experiments of herdr-manifold#5 (criterion K8 "delivery by channel",
    decision "Agents behind a protocol"): a channel registers only in an
    interactive session and, until it is on an allowlist, after a
    confirmation at every start;
  * a pane opened from an agent, read-only by default, scrolled by touch;
    typing into it is an explicit switch.
. *Images pasted on the phone* go to the hub as a blob and from there to
  Herdr as `ClipboardImage`, the way a remote client sends them. For Herdr
  the hub is always a remote client, so pasting images works for every
  machine, the hub's own included.
. *Notifications* reach the phone by the Telegram bot the author already
  uses, until Web Push of the installed app is tried.

== Choices made for the author

Each is the recommended option, taken so that the work could proceed; the
author may overturn any of them.

. *Hubs on every machine, all alike*, over one hub on ws1: no single point
  of failure, and no extra hop for a desktop that is not ws1. The price is
  that the hubs share state (the inbox, see "Open decisions").
. *NetBird for the phone*, over a public address behind a reverse proxy or
  a tunnel: nothing is exposed to the internet, and the network is already
  there.
. *DNS-01*, over an own CA installed on the phone: nothing to install, and
  renewal is unattended.
. *A view made for the phone*, over the desktop layout scaled down.

== Open decisions

* *Authentication* — passkeys (WebAuthn) bound to the hub's origin, a
  client certificate, or NetBird's identity of the peer alone. Every hub
  must accept what another issued, since the phone moves between them.
* *Shared state of the hubs* — the inbox of herdr-manifold#5, the read
  marks and the layout of the views: one store replicated between hubs, or
  each record owned by the hub of the recipient's machine and read through
  it. This closes the decision "Where the inbox lives" of herdr-manifold#5.
* *Port and names* — one port on every machine, and whether a name
  `manifold.nb.kovrov.space` is worth having next to `wsN`.
* *Sizes of panes with several clients* — Herdr renders a tab for the size
  of the client's window; what it does when a phone and a desktop watch the
  same tab is not established (research of herdr-manifold#5, section "Not
  established"). The read-only phone pane may need its own rendering from
  `pane.read` instead of the client protocol.
* *Certificate distribution* — one wildcard certificate renewed on one
  machine and copied to the others, or each hub renewing its own name.
* *Who keeps the API token for DNS-01* — the zone `kovrov.space` must allow
  a token limited to `_acme-challenge` records.

== Relation to other specifications

* herdr-manifold#1: the core and the UI are unchanged; the Electron
  application stops owning the core and becomes a shell around the local
  hub's app. Its criterion K7 ("input latency") holds for the desktop's
  own hub.
* herdr-manifold#5: the broker of messages is part of the hub, which closes
  its decision "Whether the broker is part of the client"; its decision
  "Where the inbox lives" moves here as "Shared state of the hubs".
* herdr-manifold#3: the hub is packaged in the `.deb` of ws1–ws5 with a
  user unit, and in archives for ws7 (installed in `~/.local`, its root is
  read-only) and ws9 (macOS, arm64, `launchd`); the Flatpak is the Electron
  shell only. The hub runs on the Node that ships with Electron, or on one
  bundled with an archive.

== Prior art

Ovis (`github.com/devswha/ovis`, formerly `herdr-web-ui`, MIT; Bun,
TypeScript, React, xterm.js) is a browser and phone client for Herdr:
chat and live terminal for agent panes, machines over SSH, Web Push. It
installs a bridge of its own on each machine, where this specification uses
Herdr's bridges; it has no projects across machines and no messaging
between agents. Its phone view is to be read before ours is designed
(research of herdr-manifold#5, section "Ovis"). Since 2026-11-12 it is the
author's working client (see "Decision of 2026-11-12: Ovis for now").

== Acceptance criteria

[cols="1,6"]
|===
| # | Criterion and method

| K1
| A prototype hub on ws1 and ws3, against throwaway Herdr sessions, serves
  the UI over HTTPS with a certificate obtained by DNS-01 for
  `*.nb.kovrov.space`; a browser on ws2 and on the author's phone, both in
  NetBird, opens it without a certificate warning; neither hub answers on
  a public address (checked from outside NetBird).

| K2
| From ws2's own hub, keystroke to patch into a pane on ws3: the median and
  the 95th percentile over 200 keystrokes stay within 2 ms of the direct
  SSH measurement of herdr-manifold#5 (criterion K1 there).

| K3
| Failover: with ws1's hub stopped, the phone's app moves to ws3's hub
  within 10 s, says so, and shows the same agents and messages.

| K4
| Phone view: the agents of ws1, ws2 and ws3 with their states; a message
  sent to an agent from the phone; an approval of Claude Code answered from
  the phone, once criterion K8 of herdr-manifold#5 has passed; a pane
  opened read-only and scrolled. Each checked by hand and
  recorded in research with screenshots.

| K5
| An image pasted on the phone reaches a Claude Code agent on ws1 as a file
  path, through Herdr's `ClipboardImage`.

| K6
| Without authentication every request of the hub, the WebSocket included,
  is refused.

| K7
| The open decisions above are each closed in this specification — decided,
  with the experiment that decided it, or moved to a named later
  specification.

| K8
| `scripts/check-specs.sh` passes.
|===
